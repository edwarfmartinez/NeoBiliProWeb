AAP vs NICE Bilirubin Guidelines

AAP 2022 and NICE CG98 both provide structured guidance for neonatal hyperbilirubinemia, but their scope, threshold construction, risk framing, units, and surrounding recommendations are not interchangeable.

Last reviewed: September 20, 2026

Do not choose between frameworks by selecting the more favorable threshold or convert one guideline’s logic into the other. Use the framework required by your clinical setting and apply its complete recommendations.

AAP 2022: key distinctions

The AAP guideline applies from 35 weeks’ gestation. Its treatment figures vary by gestational age and whether additional hyperbilirubinemia neurotoxicity risk factors are present. The AAP implementation FAQ clarifies that Figure 3 is used for infants 35–37 6/7 weeks only when there are additional neurotoxicity risk factors besides gestational age.

The AAP also bases post-discharge follow-up planning on the difference between the bilirubin concentration and the hour-specific phototherapy threshold, modified by other clinical considerations.

NICE CG98: key distinctions

NICE CG98 covers newborn babies under 28 days and provides a threshold table for babies of 38 weeks or more together with treatment-threshold graphs by gestational age. NICE materials conventionally display bilirubin in micromol/L.

NICE explicitly notes that reported bilirubin can vary between assays from different manufacturers and advises clinicians to consult their local pathology laboratory when interpreting threshold tables or charts.

Choosing the correct framework

  • Follow the guideline adopted by the healthcare system, institution, or service in which care is delivered.
  • Confirm that the infant is within the selected guideline’s intended population.
  • Keep units visible through data entry, interpretation, documentation, and handoff.
  • Apply that framework’s definitions of risk factors and its surrounding recommendations.
  • Consult local policy where laboratory methods, escalation pathways, or follow-up resources affect implementation.
