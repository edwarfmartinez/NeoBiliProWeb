Visual reference

Neonatal Bilirubin Nomogram

An hour-specific graph places a bilirubin measurement on a time axis and shows its relationship to clinically relevant curves. Correct interpretation starts with knowing exactly what those curves represent.

Last reviewed: September 20, 2026

What “nomogram” can mean

In neonatal bilirubin care, the word nomogram is used broadly. It may refer to an hour-specific risk-prediction plot or to treatment-threshold graphs for phototherapy and exchange transfusion. Those are not the same clinical tool.

Before interpreting any plotted result, identify the source guideline, intended population, measurement units, gestational-age assumptions, risk-factor assumptions, and whether the curves predict risk or indicate treatment thresholds.

A plotted zone is not self-explanatory.

Do not transfer a measurement from one framework to another or treat an older risk-zone label as an AAP 2022 treatment recommendation. Use the chart specified by the current guideline and local protocol.

Anatomy of a treatment-threshold graph

Graph elementWhat it representsInterpretation check
X-axisPostnatal age in hours at the time bilirubin was measured.Calculate from the recorded birth and collection times.
Y-axisBilirubin concentration in the stated unit.Confirm whether the chart uses mg/dL or micromol/L.
Patient markerThe measured bilirubin at a specific age.Verify method, timestamp, and units.
Phototherapy curveThe applicable phototherapy treatment boundary from the selected framework.Confirm gestational age and risk context.
Exchange curveThe applicable exchange transfusion boundary.Consult the full guideline, including escalation recommendations.
Serial lineA visual connection between measurements over time.Review the actual rate and intervals; visual slope depends on chart scale.

A disciplined reading sequence

  1. Name the framework. Confirm AAP 2022, NICE CG98, or another local/national guideline.
  2. Confirm eligibility. Check that the infant falls within the population covered by the selected chart.
  3. Confirm the inputs. Verify gestation, postnatal age, units, measurement method, and relevant risk factors.
  4. Locate the patient value. Read the exact age and bilirubin rather than estimating from a screenshot.
  5. Compare with the correct curves. Assess the distance and direction relative to treatment thresholds.
  6. Add trajectory and clinical context. Review prior results, rate of rise, symptoms, feeding, treatment, and follow-up feasibility.

AAP 2022 and NICE CG98

Both frameworks use bilirubin concentration, age in hours, and gestational maturity, but they differ in scope, chart construction, units commonly displayed, and how additional risk is incorporated. A visual graph does not remove those differences.

Accessible graph design matters.

Clinical status should never be communicated by color alone. Labels, line styles, values, and a written classification help preserve meaning for color-vision differences, monochrome printing, and low-quality displays.
