Guideline reference

AAP 2022 Bilirubin Thresholds

The 2022 American Academy of Pediatrics guideline uses hour-specific treatment thresholds for newborn infants 35 or more weeks’ gestation. Correct threshold selection depends on more than the bilirubin value alone.

Last reviewed: September 20, 2026

Clinical context

The AAP guideline addresses prevention, risk assessment, monitoring, and management of hyperbilirubinemia in newborn infants born at 35 weeks’ gestation or later. Its phototherapy and exchange transfusion graphs plot total serum bilirubin against postnatal age in hours.

Treatment thresholds vary with gestational age and with the presence of hyperbilirubinemia neurotoxicity risk factors. The guideline provides separate phototherapy figures for infants with and without additional neurotoxicity risk factors.

Do not select a curve from age and bilirubin alone.

Confirm gestational age, the infant’s exact postnatal age, applicable neurotoxicity risk factors, measurement method, previous treatment, and the broader clinical picture. Consult the official guideline for full definitions and recommendations.

How to interpret the graph

  1. Identify the infant’s postnatal age at the time of measurement.
  2. Select the correct gestational-age curve and the appropriate risk-factor figure.
  3. Plot the bilirubin measurement using consistent units.
  4. Assess its position relative to the phototherapy threshold, escalation-of-care threshold, and exchange transfusion threshold described by the guideline.
  5. Use the distance from the phototherapy threshold, clinical circumstances, and ability to arrange follow-up when planning reassessment.

Rate of rise in the AAP guideline

The AAP describes a bilirubin rate of increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL per hour during the first 24 hours, or at least 0.2 mg/dL per hour thereafter, as exceptional and suggestive of hemolysis. Rate of rise is therefore a signal to investigate in context—not a substitute for the treatment-threshold graphs.

Follow-up is tied to threshold distance.

The 2022 guideline changed post-discharge follow-up planning from the earlier risk-zone approach to the difference between the bilirubin concentration and the hour-specific phototherapy threshold, together with other clinical considerations.
