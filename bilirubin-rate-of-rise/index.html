Clinical concept

Bilirubin Rate of Rise

Rate of rise describes how much a bilirubin measurement changes per hour between two time-stamped results. It can help identify an unusually rapid trajectory, but it must be interpreted alongside treatment thresholds and the full clinical picture.

Last reviewed: September 20, 2026

Definition and calculation

Calculate rate of rise from two bilirubin measurements obtained at different times. Use the same analyte and units for both values, and calculate the elapsed interval from the actual collection times.

Rate of rise = (later bilirubin − earlier bilirubin) ÷ elapsed hours

For example, an increase from 12.0 to 15.0 mg/dL over 12 hours is a rise of 3.0 mg/dL divided by 12 hours, or 0.25 mg/dL per hour. This calculation alone does not determine treatment.

What to verify before calculating

CheckReason
Measurement timestampsRounded ages or dates can materially change a per-hour rate over short intervals.
Consistent unitsDo not subtract a value in mg/dL from one in micromol/L.
Measurement methodRecord whether results are total serum bilirubin or transcutaneous bilirubin and follow the selected guideline for confirmatory testing.
Treatment timingPhototherapy or other interventions between measurements change the context of the observed trajectory.
Patient contextGestational age, age in hours, risk factors, feeding, hemolysis evaluation, and clinical status remain relevant.

AAP 2022 context

The AAP guideline describes a rise of at least 0.3 mg/dL per hour during the first 24 hours, or at least 0.2 mg/dL per hour thereafter, as exceptional and suggestive of hemolysis. The guideline includes rapid increase among risk factors for developing significant hyperbilirubinemia.

A signal, not a stand-alone treatment threshold.

Use rate of rise to support assessment and further evaluation. Phototherapy and exchange decisions still depend on the applicable hour-specific treatment thresholds and clinical circumstances.

NICE CG98 context

NICE uses its own framework and units. CG98 describes bilirubin rising more than 8.5 micromol/L per hour as rapidly rising in recommendations related to intensified phototherapy and increased kernicterus risk.

The AAP and NICE values should not be detached from their surrounding recommendations or treated as interchangeable cutoffs. Record the selected guideline whenever documenting the rate.

Clinical interpretation

  • Confirm that the interval is long enough and the timestamps are reliable.
  • Review the plotted trajectory, not only the last pair of results.
  • Consider how close the current value is to the relevant phototherapy threshold.
  • Evaluate possible hemolysis and other causes when the trajectory is unexpectedly rapid.
  • Follow the official guideline and local protocol for testing, monitoring, treatment, and escalation.
